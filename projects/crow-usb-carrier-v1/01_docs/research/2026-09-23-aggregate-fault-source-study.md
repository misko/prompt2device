# Crow eight-spoke aggregate fault: source/fuse engineering study

**Disposition:** The fitted fuse, reverse-input PFET and eight TPS26625 branch eFuses can form a defensible **conditional source-stage fault envelope without a new aggregate breaker**. They do not yet prove unconditional all-fault continuity or protection with an arbitrary 12 V supply. A compatible external isolated supply may be specified by limits now and selected/qualified later. The existing E-FAULT checker only admits a programmable aggregate breaker, so its present failure is a checker/contract representation gap as well as a missing envelope; it does not itself establish a physical breaker requirement. Read-only study of clean root `7453376b`; no schema, source, or checker changes.

## Source path and applicable requirement

At J_PWR, F_IN `0451004.MRL` 4 A Nano2 precedes reverse-hookup Q_IN `DMP6023LFG-13`. Eight `TPS26625DRCR` devices, each with 44.2 kΩ ILIM and 10 nF dVdT, branch from N12V_PROTECTED. The buck is a parallel branch. `requirements.yaml` promises **eight simultaneous 100 mA, 10.8–13.2 V connector-plane outputs in normal continuous operation**. `protection_paths.yaml` additionally requires normal/startup and **one branch-limited fault** not to open F_IN; a common-trunk/shorted-TVS fault may either clear the fuse or remain within a source limit/hiccup envelope. Neither source says healthy spokes must remain above 10.8 V during five or eight simultaneous faults. That would be a new requirement, not a consequence of the normal claim.

TI SLVSDT4F §7.5 gives IOL 0.145/0.152/0.159 A at RILIM=44.2 kΩ and VIN=24 V, VIN−VOUT=1 V, over the table temperature range. **0.159 A is not a guaranteed maximum for a collapsed 12 V output.** TI §9.3.5 says overload regulates at IOL for up to current-limit delay, then turns off; TPS26625 automatically retries after a stated 512 ms. A hot short initially invokes a fast-trip comparator with **1.6 A typical** threshold and ~220 ns **typical** response, then returns to current regulation. Neither is a worst-case aggregate-current clamp. Device input/output bypasses and source/cable capacitance can add short pulses. Thus eight in-phase faults/retries cannot be proved by multiplying 0.159 A alone. ## Quantitative screen

The adopted normal input allocation is 2.123856 A including eight 100 mA spokes and eight table-conditioned 482 µA eFuse IQ terms. Replacing `k` 100 mA loads with 159 mA regulated-fault loads gives `I = 2.123856 + 0.059k` A. This is a **static planning screen, not a hard-short bound**.

| Concurrent faults | Static input A | Margin to 2.85 A hot fuse screen | Protected-bus floor at 11.4 V input, 150 mΩ ×1.2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 2.124 | 0.726 | 11.018 V |
| 1 | 2.183 | 0.667 | 11.007 V |
| 4 | 2.360 | 0.490 | 10.975 V |
| 8 | 2.596 | 0.254 | 10.933 V |

With the already allocated 171 mV per-spoke path drop, the one-fault screen still leaves a healthy connector at **10.836 V**, 36 mV above 10.8 V. The eight-fault bus floor would give 10.762 V at any hypothetical healthy spoke; therefore do **not** claim 10.8 V service during every concurrent fault. To sustain normal digital/buck allocation plus all eight regulated faults below the 2.85 A fuse screen without source foldback, each faulty branch must remain below about **0.191 A** (`(2.85−[2.123856−0.8])/8`). TI's 0.159 A table row leaves a 20% per-device engineering allowance to that threshold, but only at the table condition. If field short/retry pulses exceed it, the admissible source-limit/hiccup mode must contain them; it need not preserve service during an all-fault event.

Littelfuse's 4 A exact row lists 16 mΩ nominal cold resistance, **3.152 A²s nominal melting I²t**, 50 A/125 V interrupting, and 200%-rating opening within 5 s. These are not guaranteed non-opening I²t or a fuse trip at 2.85 A. Existing 2.85 A at 70 °C is a project hot continuous screen; the 0.254 A all-fault margin is small. Q_IN's exact Diodes DS37204 lists RDS(on) max 25 mΩ at VGS=−10 V, 6.2 A steady at 70 °C under its specified board, and thermal resistances 60 or 123 °C/W depending on copper. At the project's 50 mΩ hot allocation and 2.85 A RMS, Q_IN dissipates **0.406 W**; even the 123 °C/W static screen yields about **120 °C junction from 70 °C ambient**, below its 150 °C limit. A 4 A peak is 0.8 W at that resistance and needs a pulse/transient thermal screen. Its source path and gate-drive realization remain layout/first-article qualifications. [Littelfuse primary series PDF](/home/mouse9911/gits/circuits-worktrees/crow-usb-carrier-v1-20260922/projects/crow-usb-carrier-v1/02_parts/0451004.MRL/Littelfuse_451_453_series.pdf); [Diodes primary DS37204](/home/mouse9911/gits/circuits-worktrees/crow-usb-carrier-v1-20260922/projects/crow-usb-carrier-v1/02_parts/DMP6023LFG-13/DMP6023LFG_DS37204_Rev2-2.pdf).

At a hard short and 13.2 V, a branch held at the **table** 0.159 A would dissipate 2.10 W. TI's 44.8 °C/W JEDEC θJA would imply ~164 °C junction from 70 °C, above the **typical** 155 °C thermal-shutdown threshold. Auto-retry/thermal shutdown is therefore expected; neither continuous 0.159 A dissipation nor a 512 ms worst-case retry period should be asserted. Eight neighboring devices and cable capacitance require thermal/retry waveform qualification. This does not by itself require an upstream programmable breaker, because source limitation can bound total path stress.

## Passable external-supply envelope, subject to qualification

Use the existing **current-limit/hiccup**, not the 8–50 A fuse-clearing, option as the preferred no-new-breaker path. A compliant isolated supply and cable assembly shall deliver **11.4–13.2 V at J_PWR at 2.185 A continuously** for normal and one static branch-limited fault, without hiccup; never exceed 13.2 V on recovery. For any multi-spoke short, simultaneous retry, or common-trunk/TVS fault, require a bounded current/load-line at J_PWR with **≤4 A peak** including output-capacitor discharge and cable ringing, **≤2.85 A RMS over the applicable fuse/PFET thermal window at 70 °C**, and **≤1.576 A²s effective fuse-heating budget per isolated pulse or pulse train**, including retry overlap and incomplete cooldown. At a rectangular 4 A pulse, this budget alone would limit duration to 98.5 ms; repeated pulses cannot each consume that allowance without thermal recovery. The source must remain stable under the buck and eight auto-retrying eFuses, and return to the valid input window after faults clear. The exact pulse-train window/cooldown and local post-fuse capacitor discharge belong in first-article coordination. A supply meeting these conditions can cap path stress even when per-spoke hard-short current and fast-trip peaks are not separately bounded by TI's IOL table.

The alternative **fuse-clearing source mode** in current `protection_paths.yaml` is not yet passable for this board. At its minimum sustained 8 A and the 50 mΩ Q_IN allocation, Q_IN would dissipate **3.2 W** until clearing, while Littelfuse only gives up to 5 s at 8 A. Q_IN transient thermal survival and copper/fuse energy have not been shown for that interval. Do not treat the allowed text as qualified hardware. Restrict the new engineering envelope to the source-limit/hiccup mode unless a separate 8–50 A clearing coordination proof is supplied.

The essential unresolved external fact is **the actual isolated source plus cable output load-line and fault waveform**: peak including stored-capacitor discharge, thermal-window RMS, retry/hiccup pulse width and spacing, recovery overshoot, and ability to maintain 11.4 V at 2.185 A. It may be specified as a procurement/compliance envelope now; an exact supply SKU is not needed to decide the source architecture. Also qualify actual TPS26625 IOL/fast-trip/retry overlap at 11.4–13.2 V and temperature, all-pod load/cable capacitance, and hot F_IN/Q_IN/PCB path. These are measurable first-article and supplier-compliance gates, not grounds to invent a fixed-breaker threshold.

**Smallest justified source work before layout:** author the conditional all-spoke source-limit/hiccup envelope and its explicit service-vs-fault behavior in `power_tree.yaml`/`protection_paths.yaml`, bind the eight exact fitted ILIM/dVdT values, and procure/qualify a supply to it. Then give E-FAULT a separately reviewed fuse-plus-external-source coordination representation; its present programmer/timer-only shape cannot describe this valid architecture. Do not add a breaker merely to pass that schema. If a compliant supply/cable cannot satisfy the peak/RMS/I²t and 2.185 A voltage boundaries, or if a new requirement demands uninterrupted 10.8 V service through all simultaneous faults, revisit aggregate hardware and the full trunk/dropout budget.
