## Correct temperature boundary

TI gives PCM4204 an operating temperature range **−10 to +70 °C**. The `−40..+80 °C` previously used to reject it is in the *remote roof-array spoke* `projects/crow-roof-array-v1/03_src/rules/spoke_interface.yaml:87–88`, not in the carrier's agreed `01_docs/BRIEF.md`. That brief locks spoken connector electrical compatibility but has no carrier board ambient range or enclosure location. Therefore temperature is an **open carrier deployment requirement**, not an existing hard rejection. A carrier expected outside −10..+70 °C would reject PCM4204; the stated spoke limit alone does not. No extension of TI's operating range is claimed.

## Signal/noise with the actual pod

PCM4204 differential full-scale is 6.0 Vpp = **2.121 Vrms**. The retained 1.2 Vrms differential connector maximum is 4.95 dB below full scale, assuming the analog transfer remains unity. TI specifies at 48 kHz **118 dB typical, 112 dB minimum dynamic range**, measured with a −60 dBFS 1 kHz sine, A-weighted, 22 Hz high-pass and 20 kHz low-pass; all tabular dynamic tests are at 25 °C in master mode. Inferred input-referred planning noise: `2.121/10^(118/20) = 2.67 µVrms` typical; `2.121/10^(112/20) = 5.33 µVrms` using the minimum DR row (not a guaranteed noise limit over temperature or in TDM slave mode). At the nominal pod 1 Pa/94 dBSPL connector output 0.1033 Vrms, capsule 80 dB SNR gives ~10.33 µVrms capsule noise. ADC-only penalty is `10log10[1+(2.67/10.33)^2] = 0.28 dB` typical, or 1.02 dB using the 112 dB row. Add AFE/filter/cable noise in quadrature on the same bandwidth and weighting before deciding. TI's −105 dB typical THD+N is a −0.5 dBFS 1 kHz result; Crow 1.2 Vrms max is −4.95 dBFS, so do not transplant that exact distortion number to Crow without characterization. This is materially quieter than TLV320ADC6140 DRE-off 2.5 kΩ's ~4.48 µVrms planning estimate, while TLV's DRE-on weak-signal mode may be quieter still.

## Eight-channel clock/data architecture and coherence limit

TI explicitly draws **two PCM4204 devices** driving a common TDM bus (Figure 7): one `SUB=0`, one `SUB=1`; in long-frame TDM Figure 4 each produces four 24-bit two's-complement samples in 32-bit slots, first chip slots 1–4, second slots 5–8, with the least significant eight slot bits don't-care. At 48 kHz, share **24.576 MHz SCKI = 512fs** from the retained `Y_AUDIO` oscillator/clock gate, **12.288 MHz BCK = 256fs** and **48 kHz LRCK/FSYNC** generated by the XU316, and a common active-low `RST`; configure both PCM4204s as slaves, single-rate, TDM long-frame with the same no-delay or one-BCK-delay frame selection. `crow_usb_digital.tsx:312–317,344–353` shows this oscillator and the XU-to-ADC BCK/FSYNC path; the revised two-load clock fanout and skew remain to be checked. TI permits 256fs SCKI as well; retained 24.576 MHz is a valid mode. The 12.288 MHz BCK is below TI's 27.648 MHz TDM maximum. The LRCK start-of-frame is its rising edge; data changes on BCK falling edges, requiring the XU receive edge/polarity and slot map to match. The PCM4204 is **hardware-pin configured**, so no I²C/SPI control link or new register-initialization firmware is needed; XU USB/audio firmware remains a separate pre-existing contract, not authored here.

Common SCKI/LRCK/BCK and `RST` are necessary for coherent operation; the datasheet says external reset initializes after 1024 SCKI cycles and specifies a 37/fs linear-phase decimator group delay. Its two-chip TDM figure proves bus serialization but does **not** state a maximum cross-device aperture mismatch or guarantee reset-independent decimator phase alignment. TI's EVM also carries two PCM4204s and a common low-jitter clock, but it is not a cross-chip phase guarantee. Close this by a TI-supported synchronization statement/procedure or an independent hardware timing proof, then measure repeated cold/warm resets and simultaneous impulses across all eight channels. Do not equate identical slot frames with proven analog phase.

## Front end and power: substantial backtrack

TI's analog input impedance is **3.0 kΩ average per pin**, full scale 6 Vpp differential, and its suggested VCOM bias output is **+2.5 V**, buffered only for >1 MΩ external nodes unless a follower is used. Existing `crow_retained_analog.tsx` OPA2320 outputs run on quiet 3.3 V with ~1.65 V common mode, then pass through TMUX2821 and **10 kΩ ADC-pin ground pulldowns + 1 nF shunts**; existing pod 1 µF caps are before the OPA, so the OPA-to-ADC path is DC-coupled. With 3 kΩ ADC input and 10 kΩ pulldown the approximate per-leg AC load is 2.31 kΩ, excluding filter/mux, and the 2.5 V converter bias and ground pulldown cause DC loading. The current pin network cannot be relabeled as PCM4204-compatible. Rework or remove pulldowns, prove the isolation-off state, differential DC offset/current, OPA output swing and THD into the actual load, and use the ADC's 2.5 V common mode appropriately. AC-coupling each of 16 legs near the ADC is a plausible way to isolate the 1.65 V OPA bias from 2.5 V converter bias, but capacitor value, 20 Hz amplitude/phase, effective capacitance, dielectric THD and placement remain design choices, not a prescribed 22 µF count. TI's EVM circuit uses OPA1632 fully differential amplifiers and external ±15 V rails; this is an example, **not** proof that the existing 3.3 V OPA2320 is sufficient or that ±15 V is mandatory.

The converter itself requires **4.75–5.25 V analog at 108 mA typical/130 mA max per chip** and **3.0–3.6 V digital at 18 mA typical/23 mA max per chip** at 48 kHz: two chips add up to 260 mA max on a **qualified low-noise analog rail** and 46 mA max on digital. The datasheet does **not** mandate an LDO or exactly 5.000 V. Existing `5V_BUCK` is specified 4.95–5.05 V, within the voltage range; a filtered branch from it is a legitimate candidate if ripple/noise, impedance, load transients, isolation from USB/core switching and retained shutdown/hold-up behavior can be demonstrated at the ADC pins. Existing LT3045 regulates only `3V3_ADC`; `5V_LDO_HOLD` is a precharged/hold-up node, not a qualified quiet analog 5 V source by name alone. An optional separate >5 V preregulator from protected 12 V followed by a low-noise LDO could give stronger isolation, but it is **not an established necessity** and would need stocked exact parts, thermal/headroom and sequencing proof. Direct 12→5 V linear regulation would dissipate roughly `(12−5)×0.26 = 1.82 W` at maximum analog current, a significant thermal design burden. PCM4204 requires local VCC/VDD and **four reference-pair 0.1 µF + 33 µF low-ESR bypass networks across two chips** per TI Figure 15, plus VCOM handling. Power and reference backtrack exceeds the 3.3 V-only TLV320ADC6140 candidate, but the exact topology remains open. If a qualified 4.75–5.25 V analog rail cannot be implemented within the stock and thermal policy, the option fails despite its ADC stock.

## Disposition

**Keep PCM4204PAPR alive as a serious engineering comparator, not an adopted part.** It passes current public-stock arithmetic, has a genuine TI-drawn two-chip eight-slot 48 kHz TDM path, needs only hardware straps for converter setup, and at the nominal pod reference has favorable fixed-gain noise. The prior temperature dismissal was based on the wrong board's service envelope. The two decision-critical open items are: (1) whether carrier ambient actually fits −10..+70 °C and (2) whether a qualified 4.75–5.25 V analog rail plus compatible low-distortion input network can be demonstrated without compromising the existing OPA/spoke/hold-up architecture. A filtered branch of the existing buck is a candidate; a new preregulator/LDO is only an optional implementation. Cross-chip phase bounds, XU receive edge, package footprint/thermal pad and JLC exact placement acceptance remain source-stage checks. PCM4204 should not be chosen from its 118 dB headline alone, but it should not be excluded solely by remote-spoke temperature.
