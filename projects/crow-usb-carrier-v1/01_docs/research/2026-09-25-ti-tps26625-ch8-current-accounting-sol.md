# TPS26625 channel-8 input current accounting — bounded source review

This is an accounting review, not a route, width exception, ampacity result, or P1/P2 admission. Authority is the current `03_src/rules/{nets,power_tree,protection_paths}.yaml`, `02_parts/TPS26625DRCR/part.yaml`, and the retained TI [SLVSDT4F TPS2662 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2662.pdf) (local SHA-256 `ff038eaa557fb618f93c0b6276a5ae08d0d3d9fbff1dddf0c45974a89120aefa`, electrical table and §§9.3.3, 9.3.5, 9.3.9, 10.4–11). The prior [U.1 launch screen](2026-09-25-ti-tps26625-input-pad-launch-sol/README.md) establishes native geometric/DRC contact only.

## Segment boundary and present allocation

`N12V_PROTECTED` is a **single electrical net**, fed by J_PWR/F_IN/Q_IN and supplying the buck plus eight TPS26625 inputs and their 100-nF bypass capacitors. Copper *upstream of a realized fanout* carries the sum of downstream loads and fault currents. The route from a realized fanout to `U_SPOKE8.1` (and its local `C_SPOKE_IN8.1`) carries **channel-8 input current**, including that capacitor's recharge/transients and device bias. The capacitor can also deliver local fault-edge current, so its position and return path matter. The present unrouted placement has no verified fanout boundary, shared-length census, or proof that the diagnostic U.1-to-C_IN segment is exclusively downstream of the other taps.

The governed `INPUT_TRUNK` class assigns **1.2 mm minimum / 0.2 mm clearance / 2.85 A** to *every* segment of `N12V_PROTECTED`; `nets.yaml` explicitly calls this a preliminary width floor, with exact-board hot ampacity/drop owed. `power_tree.yaml` allocates 2.12 A normal aggregate load, 2.123856 A after its table-conditioned eight-device quiescent screen, and 2.185 A rounded one-branch-fault delivery. The latter follows replacing one 0.100-A pod with its 0.159-A limit: 2.123856 − 0.100 + 0.159 = **2.182856 A**, rounded upward. The conditional external-source episode permits 3.4 A instantaneous peak at J_PWR, at most 10 ms cumulative above 2.85 A, and 2.85 A persistent fault current; the specific source/cable and post-fuse capacitor discharge remain unqualified. These are *source-entry* bounds, not a measured per-spoke waveform.

For the U.1 branch, the normal output allocation is **0.100 A**; the 44.2-kΩ ILIM electrical-table overload row is **0.145 / 0.152 / 0.159 A** at VIN=24 V and VIN−VOUT=1 V (min/typ/max under the table conditions). It is a useful conditional regulated-fault screen, **not a guaranteed all-state maximum** at 11.4–13.2 V or a bound on fast shorts. TI lists a **1.6-A fast-trip threshold** and **220-ns turn-off delay** for the stated high-VIN/large-drop case as typical single values, without a guaranteed worst-case peak or pulse charge. TI describes a short initial surge before current-limit regulation. On overload the switch may regulate until thermal shutdown or its timer; with fitted 10-nF dVdT, TI's estimate `512 ms + 3.3×10 nF/(2 µA)` is about **528.5 ms**, conditional on its operating mode, not a guaranteed continuous 0.159-A interval. TPS26625 retries after about **512 ms**; after thermal shutdown the retry waits until junction cools below its hysteresis threshold. Startup slew, output capacitance/load, repeated faults, all-spoke coincidence, bypass recharge, source impedance, and local capacitor-discharge current are not bounded by the 0.159-A row. If 0.159 A happened to persist across a fully blocked output at the project's 13.2-V upper operating input, the switch would dissipate about **2.10 W** before thermal limiting; this is an illustration, not a proven current or thermal bound at 13.2 V.

## Source decision and closure evidence

**No current source-rule relaxation is justified.** Keep `INPUT_TRUNK` at 1.2 mm/2.85 A for all `N12V_PROTECTED` geometry until a governed *segment-level* current contract identifies the actual fanout/taps, aggregate versus individual downstream sets, DC normal/fault and transient/retry envelopes, capacitor charge/discharge paths, and return branches. A future lower-current local branch rule would need to bind those segments to native copper topology and keep the existing aggregate rule upstream, with checker coverage for each neck, via, pad launch, and joint. Merely reclassifying the whole net or treating the 0.159-A row as its ampacity would be unsafe. The current diagnostic full-width 1.20-mm U.1 launch is geometrically legal but has no ampacity or thermal qualification.

To close the local input path, obtain: actual routed lengths/widths/thickness and neck/via/pad/solder-contact geometry; extracted hot resistance and DC drop for common and channel segments against the 52-mΩ upstream copper/via/joint allocation and each spoke's 810-mΩ PCB allocation; simultaneous-load and fault/retry waveform at the fanout and U.1 including capacitor inrush/discharge; copper/board temperature rise at 70°C ambient and transient `I²t`; and TI junction/PowerPAD-to-RTN thermal network with cooling between retries. The exact U.1 land-to-trace effective contact, solder fillet, fabrication tolerance, current crowding, and resulting local heat are unknown even where native DRC reports connection. Maintain GND6 and RTN5/PowerPAD11 as separate networks while measuring the input-bypass and thermal returns. No width, voltage-drop, temperature, or pad-contact adequacy claim follows from this review.
