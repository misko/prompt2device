## Thermal applicability and practical prototype margin

I checked the retained Vishay P034 page-2 footnote: typical 55 K/W junction-to-air and 17 K/W junction-to-lead are conditional on 14 × 14 mm copper areas. They are not guaranteed actual-board maxima; 17 must not be added to the already whole-device 55. The SS34 pulsed 25 °C forward-drop entry is 0.5 V, not the 0.75 V belonging to other family members; the retained 0.75 V cold planning allowance is intentionally unchanged.

My favorable judgment is **not** “area ≥196 therefore 55” or “2 × 55 is automatically safe.” Each terminal now has a compact, local, directly attached outer heat-spreading region with the reference's characteristic dimensions, an exact full-land broadening witness, unchanged 35 µm outer copper and underlying continuous planes. This supplies the previously absent physical applicability premise: heat can spread in broad outer copper before traversing dielectric. Both terminals have this path, so cooling does not require an invented equal two-lead heat split. The non-centered shapes, mask, package injection, real convection and shared heating remain different from an unspecified maker coupon. A deliberately degraded **110 K/W whole-device engineering allocation** is credible for this prototype geometry, but is neither measured nor analytically bounded. No selected Isola laminate or guaranteed FR4 conductivity is invented.

I independently rederived all twelve final scalar cases and neighboring losses. The selected 110 K/W and +20 °C board/lead offset yield **131.975 °C**, or **18.025 °C** below the 150 °C absolute maximum. That is useful prototype headroom under the stated assumptions, not a universal operating target or a newly imposed 125 °C gate. At 130/+20 the estimate is 141.425 °C, only 8.575 °C headroom; at 150/+20 it is 150.875 °C and fails even the absolute boundary. These stress cases limit confidence rather than disappear behind the favorable disposition.

Neighboring heat is explicitly nonzero: U10's retained 5.25 V ×0.300 A /70% model gives 0.675 W loss; F1's maker nominal 0.153 Ω gives 0.0607257 W, or 0.1214514 W at doubled resistance. The latter is a sensitivity, not a guaranteed hot resistance. I checked the P001 maker table and P029 Rev9/2025 operating information. U10 and F1 are about 17/19 mm from D3; their bodies and other terminals also dissipate heat, and no thermal tab or ground pin is newly joined to a supply lobe. Zero coupling would be optimistic; assigning all neighboring loss directly to D3 would also be unsupported. The +20 °C allocation reasonably acknowledges substantial mutual board heating for a prototype, without claiming a solved coupling resistance. The 60 °C term is **local measured air at D3**, including warmed enclosure air; +20 is not duplicate enclosure-air heating. Restricted ventilation or worse physical coupling can invalidate the allocation.

No pre-bench guaranteed maximum is required merely because this is a prototype. Nevertheless later testing is not a waiver of missing design rationale: the broad actual copper supplies a present design basis here. Retain full-load steady-state falsification at 60 °C local air, actual loss/air/board/lead measurements and calibrated junction inference, together with existing U10 case≤90 °C and input≤0.630 A checks. Reopen engineering if the combined model is exceeded; no firmware duty, channel, ambient or current reduction is approved. No lifetime or measured-operational certainty is claimed.
