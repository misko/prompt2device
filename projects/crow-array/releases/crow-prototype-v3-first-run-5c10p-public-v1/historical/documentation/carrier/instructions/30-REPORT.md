## Compact thermal rationale and practical margin

Primary anchor: retained [Vishay 88751 SS32–SS36](https://www.vishay.com/docs/88751/ss32.pdf), revision23-Apr-2020, exact P034 PDF SHA256 `6042805475958630851bf8f949f4c0c3d9aa672c8c0a321645b7626177660288`. Its 55 K/W junction-to-air and17 K/W junction-to-lead are **typical**, with the same14 ×14 mm copper-area footnote. The17 is not added to55, which would double-count a package path. The0.75 V planning allowance remains even though the SS34 table has a lower pulsed25 °C entry.

Why this candidate is materially different from FR7: both supply terminals now directly feed compact outer copper regions exceeding196 mm², each with an exact full-width broadening witness. There is no restrictive long feed neck between the land and the credited area. The initial in-plane spreading can occur in the35 µm outer copper before heat crosses dielectric, rather than asking the small8.196 mm² land alone to drive the inner plane. Copper remains local to the diode, not hundreds of millimeters away in the power network. The regions are not literal centered squares, so their larger total area is **not** an automatic55 K/W entitlement. Mask coverage, non-square perimeter, actual convection, package variation, solder/injection spreading and neighboring heat remain relevant.

The 110 K/W allocation is deliberately pessimistic relative to the maker reference, informed by those comparable local conduction dimensions and full-land attachment. It reserves substantial deterioration rather than claiming identical boundary conditions. The 130 K/W case adds further cooling/shape degradation. These are **reference-informed engineering allocations**, not an analytical derivation of actual theta, actual-board measurements, a guarantee, or a sourced multiplier. The physical applicability argument is the actual compact full-land heat path plus reference-sized connected copper; the multiplier alone would not establish that argument. No equal two-lead heat split is assumed: both terminals receive sufficient direct cooling geometry, and the whole-device allocation includes uncertain dominant-lead behavior. No Isola laminate identity or guaranteed FR4 conductivity is asserted.

[TI SPRA953D](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/spra953d/spra953d.pdf) and [SLUA566A](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slua566a/slua566a.pdf), retained in the frozen FR7 evidence directory, support board-dependent thermal judgment and attention to shared-plane heat and cooling boundaries. Their IC/exposed-pad numerical examples are not transferred to this diode. The physical neighbor allocation reflects two specific modest-power sources rather than an unexplained offset: U10's modeled 0.675 W is generated in an elevated converter body centered about 17 mm from D3; F1's roughly 0.06–0.12 W is about 19 mm away. The new regions do not contact a converter thermal tab or ground pin; they share the existing supply connection and board. Heat leaves through the converter body/air and its other terminals as well as this board path, so assigning all its loss directly to the D3 land would be a different unsupported model. Conversely, zero mutual board heating is not credible. The +20 °C nominal allowance deliberately credits a substantial board gradient in this local region and remains subject to the existing U10 case≤90 °C falsifier. It is a reference-informed engineering allocation, not a measurement or proof of cross-coupling resistance. **60 °C means measured local air at D3**, already including warmed enclosure air; the additional offset represents mutual board/lead heating, not a second ambient-air increment. No new airflow, enclosure or mechanical requirement is added.
