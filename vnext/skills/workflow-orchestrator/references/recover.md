# Recover a failed attempt

Reopen the failing predicate and exact subject before choosing another action.
Check the predicate's requirement authority too; a reviewer-added constraint
is a proposed requirement, not automatically a defect in the candidate.
Distinguish a design defect, missing evidence, tool failure and a demonstrated
checker limitation. An unexplained result stays unresolved.

At a failed, stalled or unfinished checkpoint, the worker returns the blocking
evidence, its causal requirement or design choice, and feasible next actions.
The coordinator records an explicit decision in the existing journal: continue
a coherent unfinished attempt, repair locally, backtrack to an upstream choice,
escalate a focused question, or pause. Give the reason, owner, next discriminating
check, affected evidence and remaining budget before dispatching more work.
Neither an unfinished checkpoint nor a failed review automatically means stop
or local patch. A small value error does not justify restarting the architecture.

## Milestone plateau despite local success

Apply when a parent attempt returns, regardless of local outcome. Record helper
results as they finish; further bounded helpers under the same live parent may
proceed within its existing deadline. Keep those results pending until the parent
and all its helpers settle, then assess them together before another attempt.
Unrelated pending results still block dispatch. In the
existing journal bind the target deliverable, all newly settled results and exact
evidence. Distinguish verified milestone conditions closed without regressions
from useful prerequisites or unchanged results. The domain owns that judgment;
the execution adapter enforces the recorded transition, not engineering truth.

After one attempt without deliverable advancement, require an explicit
reassessment before dispatch. After two consecutive such checkpoints, refuse
routine continuation: choose a materially different strategy, causal backtrack,
focused escalation, or pause. State the evidence, causal hypothesis and next
discriminating check. A non-pause recovery needs a finite elapsed-time deadline
covering its prerequisite work and reviews; further reassessments cannot renew
it while the milestone remains stalled. Exhaustion stops new dispatch.

Tooling success and uncertainty reduced are useful but do not reset the plateau.
Count author/diagnosis checkpoints, not independent review or helper work again
as separate author attempts. Assess their results too; child success cannot clear
the parent milestone. Task/model/worker names, notes, budget increases and handoffs
do not reset history. Verified domain advancement clears the plateau, not spent
budget, failed evidence or same-decision repair ceilings. This is not stage
acceptance, a second ledger or permission to weaken checks. If the adapter lacks
the gate, expose that capability gap rather than claim mechanical enforcement.

## Stagnation checkpoint

Count author attempts against the underlying unresolved decision, including
incomplete/timeboxed attempts; independent review is not another author attempt.
Prerequisite research belongs to that decision's history and spend, not a fresh
retry allowance. Do not count unrelated successful tasks as failures of it.

After the first unsuccessful attempt, compare the actual evidence gained with
the closure condition. Allow a second only when a specific correction or new
discriminating check has a credible path to closure within the existing budget.
If the first result already refutes the approach or only repeats known gaps,
backtrack, escalate or pause immediately; two attempts are a ceiling, not a quota.

After at most two unsuccessful attempts on that decision, stop routine retries.
The orchestrator must record a short reassessment in the existing journal:
what changed (if anything), why the approach is stalled, the earliest causal
choice or missing fact, and the selected different action with owner, check,
affected dependencies and remaining budget. Challenge both the design and the
requirement/evidence threshold before blaming unavailable external data.
Choose a materially different upstream approach, a focused expert diagnosis,
recovery of a demonstrated tool/evidence fault, or pause for the missing authority
or fact. Another worker, larger context, more detail or renamed prerequisite is
not a different approach. A provider failure is not a circuit contradiction.

Reassessment is not permission for a third unchanged attempt or a budget reset.
A new path needs a different causal hypothesis and new evidence or a changed
designer-owned choice; keep the cumulative decision history and obtain any
required allowance/authority before dispatch. Do not repeatedly escalate the
same unanswered question. Preserve acceptance thresholds and user phase stops.
This is a coordinator-enforced rule, not an automatic scheduler or counter.

| Cause | Action |
|---|---|
| Current source can express the remedy | Repair within scope, regenerate and reverify |
| Input/decision is wrong upstream | Backtrack to its owner; invalidate affected dependents |
| Required evidence or human choice is absent | Persist the precise blocker and next action |
| Reasoning remains unresolved after bounded diagnosis | Escalate a focused question with evidence |
| Tool/checker defect is demonstrated | Repair with regression evidence; regrade the original subject |

When progress stalls over requirement interpretation, evidence sufficiency or
design approach, use an authorized, budgeted expert review before declaring an
external blocker. Ask it to distinguish actual requirements, assumptions and
unresolved interpretations; identify what the domain requires now versus permits
for later qualification; and name the next check that would change the decision.
Do not use escalation to invent unavailable facts or repeat an already resolved
audit without new evidence. A straightforward missing fact or local error does
not require an expert. If escalation is unavailable or unbudgeted, record that
limit and pause or pursue independent in-scope work. Review the expert's exact
conclusion independently before using it; expertise does not confer acceptance.

Before another attempt, name a causal hypothesis and a check whose possible
outcomes lead to different decisions. State the unresolved decision, its closure
condition and evidence threshold under the owning acceptance contract. Separate
missing evidence (uncertainty), evidence refuting the candidate (contradiction),
and later qualification of an otherwise coherent bounded candidate. A new screen
must resolve that decision or a necessary prerequisite; its completion alone
does not close the decision. Repeating the same work without new evidence is not
progress. Keep the failed result, not just the latest summary.

When a repair adds compensating circuitry or controls, compare it with changing
the earliest designer-owned cause, including removing unnecessary complexity.
Prefer the simpler supported mechanism, not necessarily the fewest parts; preserve
actual requirements and obtain authority for an out-of-scope upstream change.

Backtrack to the earliest causal choice, not automatically the previous stage.
Coupled choices may need one subsystem owner. Restart architecture selection
when foundational choices warrant it, but retain requirements, useful evidence
and rejected alternatives. A different path is not permission to reopen user
constraints, erase spent effort or execute an unsupported stage.

When a local repair fails because its changed output interacts with retained
neighbors, reassess the integration boundary before another patch. Give one
owner the complete affected functions and shared dependencies, with a check for
their preservation or supported replacement. Reopen only affected acceptance;
local tooling success does not close the parent deliverable. Domain contracts
define those functions and checks; the orchestrator must not invent them.

Preserve the domain binding's per-attempt timebox; it is not a total decision
deadline. Apply its retry limits to the unresolved decision and track research
spend cumulatively, including prerequisite investigations and candidate screens. If the
binding supplies no total decision research budget, declare one before dispatch.
A changed model, worker, report, schema, task name or split into subtasks does
not reset retries or spend; record both in the existing decision history.
Budget exhaustion requires reassessment or pause, not automatic enlargement.
No escalation permits weaker checks, hidden scope expansion or continuation
past a user checkpoint.

Escalation packets contain the question, relevant constraints, failed attempts,
current hypothesis, exact evidence and a bounded deliverable. Prefer a targeted
diagnosis to assigning the entire project again.

Backtracking creates a new upstream attempt, preserves history and marks
affected downstream evidence stale until reopened. Never patch generated
evidence or refresh an independent approval merely to obtain a pass.
